1. Information service

PREZLO supplies information about observed activity in U.S. stocks. It does not tell you whether to buy, sell or hold a security, predict a future move, assess suitability or execute transactions. No advisory, brokerage or fiduciary relationship is created by a subscription. This describes the service offered; it is not a claim of regulatory exemption or approval.

An alert means that PREZLO's data-processing conditions were met. It does not establish the cause of a move, a company's value, a favourable opportunity or the likelihood that a move will continue. It may concern volatile, illiquid or otherwise risky securities.

Alert timing. An alert is sent after unusual activity has already begun, sometimes after the price has already risen substantially. By the time an alert is received and opened, the price may differ materially from the price at detection, and buying after an alert may mean buying near the top of the move. Securities that appear in alerts, particularly low-priced and thinly traded stocks, can rise sharply and then fall sharply within the same day, including right after the regular session opens following a pre-market alert, and can close below the price at which they were detected. Low liquidity may also prevent buying or selling at the quantity or price you expect.

2. Data and delivery

Data comes from external market-data sources and is processed by PREZLO. Detection, sampling, processing, dispatch and receipt are different times. Quotes, trades, aggregates and derived figures are not interchangeable. A “live” screen does not guarantee an executable current price.

Data may be delayed, missing, stale or incorrect. Network issues, market halts, limited liquidity and source corrections can affect the display. The last received price may remain visible during a halt or interruption. A fallback data source can have a different timestamp or delay. Displayed peak timestamps may reflect when the system recorded the value rather than the exact exchange trade time. Retrospective downward corrections are not guaranteed.

Generic push notifications do not identify the security. Viewing stock details requires opening the app and having the necessary access. Delivery is not guaranteed at the same instant for every recipient.

3. Understanding the historical display

The historical record is a record of detected market movements, not a record of executed trades or customer returns.

The baseline used for the displayed movement is the low of the detection minute's price bar. It is not necessarily the market price when the push was sent or received, or a price available to you. The recorded peak is the highest price recorded from the time of detection until the end of that day's observation window, generally 16:00 New York time (the window opens at 04:00). Source timing and fallback limitations described above can affect it.

The displayed peak percentage measures the difference between that recorded peak and baseline divided by the baseline. It does not subtract spread, commissions, slippage, taxes or financing costs. Buying at the baseline and selling at the peak may have been impossible.

What the displayed percentage does not mean. The percentage shown with an alert or in the record, including in wording such as “since we found it”, describes only the gap between the baseline and the highest price recorded from detection until the end of that day's observation window. It is not the day's price change, the closing price, or a profit that was made or could have been made. Even on a day with a high displayed peak percentage, the stock may have closed below the baseline and may have kept falling on later days, in some cases losing almost all of its value. Someone who bought after detection, especially near the peak, could have lost money even on an entry that shows a high peak percentage.

The peak-percentage display has a zero floor. A 0% entry therefore does not show the full size of a decline. A decline label is applied only under particular conditions and does not identify every loss or every risky security. “Peak” is not a promise of an available sale price.

The historical window ordinarily covers approximately 60 trading days and includes the current session only after the regular session has closed (16:00 New York time). It is not an exhaustive record of every stock move or an independent audit. Display order, selected dates and highlights can change what is visible. A selected example is not evidence of a typical outcome. The record may list the largest moves first and highlight the biggest ones, and summaries or examples shown in the app, in notifications or in our marketing may feature the largest moves of a period. This makes large moves more visible than typical ones; many alerts move far less, and many fall after detection.

Bull and bear figures. The bull and bear numbers on alert cards are not a count of user votes alone. They combine votes cast by users with an estimate that PREZLO adds to each card, and the app labels them as an estimate. The estimate grows with the size of the move and the time since detection, and the share of bulls in it rises with the size of the move. These figures are not a survey of investors, a forecast or a measure of market sentiment, and you should not rely on them as one.
